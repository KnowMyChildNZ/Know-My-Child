The missing middle

The children the system was never built to catch.

A child who holds it together at school and unravels at home is not a contradiction in the evidence. It is the finding.

Nobody sees the whole child. Home sees one child, school sees another, and both accounts are true. That gap is not a problem to resolve. It is the most useful thing either of them knows.

One in five children in New Zealand is neurodivergent. Five or six in every classroom. They are not less capable. They learn differently.

Most will never meet the threshold for a formal assessment or funded support, and recognition of a child's needs currently arrives long before any meaningful support does.

Whatever the profile, the experience is often the same. They are working harder than almost any other child in the room. Not because the work is harder, but because everything around the work is harder. The noise. The pace. The transitions. The effort of holding it all together while also trying to learn.

And then the school day ends, and it comes apart at the door. The teacher reports a child who is doing fine. The family is living something else entirely.

The adults are working just as hard. Parents trying everything and running out of things to try. Teachers using the approaches that work for everyone else and watching them not land. Nobody is failing. What works for most children simply does not work for this one, and no one can yet say why.

Somewhere along the way, the child stops believing the problem is with the system. They start believing the problem is with them.

What we build

How can you personalise learning for a child you don't yet understand?

That question is the whole argument. Understanding is not a feature alongside the others. It is the thing everything else depends on, so we build it first.

One. Understand

A whole-child profile

The picture above, built from what the child shows us and what the adults around them each see. Their strengths named first, then what they find hard and why. Understanding, never a diagnosis.

In build now.

Two. Support

Strategies that fit this child

The profile turns into specific things to try, at home and in the classroom. Parents and teachers add what they are seeing and what is working, so the picture grows and changes as the child does. Reports and assessments you already hold can live alongside it.

Next.

Three. Personalise

Learning shaped to the child

Only once the first two are real does personalised learning mean anything. The curriculum reshaped to reach a particular child, at their level and their pace, through what they love. Human led, technology supported. Not more screen time, and only for those who want it.

Later.

Most tools adapt to what a child does. We adapt to who the child is.

Who it is for

Built for every child, and especially the ones who experience the world differently.

With or without a diagnosis, and whether or not one is ever wanted.

Finally, something that helps me understand my child.

You know your child's strengths, and you can see where things are not working. You may not want a clinical label, and you may not be able to get one anyway. What you want is to understand why the mornings are hard, why an ordinary afternoon can undo them, and why the thing that worked last week does not work today.

We help you make sense of what is underneath, then give you practical steps built around your child rather than generic advice. Things to try at home, and clear language to take to their teacher when you need to be understood too.

Why we are building this

Understanding doesn't change a child. It changes the people around them.

From very early in my daughter's life, we noticed how differently she experienced the world. In a way it was hard for her simply to be in the world. She took it in so differently from most people around her, and that was challenging, every day. But she also had this extraordinary spark, and we noticed that too.

What I have learned since is that when the people around a child finally see who that child really is, they can hear what the behaviour has been trying to tell them. Support follows from that. So many children are not one thing. They are ahead in some areas and genuinely finding others hard, all at once, and the strategies usually offered were never built for the way they experience the world.

The earlier a child is understood, the sooner support can come and thriving can begin. That is why this exists.

Kas Craw, Founder

How we work

Built with care, on principle.

Non-diagnostic by design

We explain how a child learns and what helps. We do not diagnose, screen for conditions, or apply labels, and the product is not built to.

Your family owns the data

The profile belongs to the child's family. You choose what is shared and with whom, you can withdraw it, and it is never sold to anyone.

Privacy Act 2020

Designed from the start to align with New Zealand privacy law, with consent gathered separately for any research use.

Home counts as evidence

What a family sees in the evenings carries the same weight as what a teacher sees at eleven in the morning. Neither account is the whole child on its own.

Evidence before claims

Where we do not yet know something, the profile says so. Every statement in it can be traced back to what it was based on.

Built in Aotearoa

Designed here, with a Te Ao Māori lens and equity at the core rather than added afterwards.

Understanding shouldn't depend on a diagnosis, a waitlist, or what a family can afford.

We are building a school-initiated route so cost is never the reason a child goes unknown, alongside a model where families who can pay help fund free access for those who cannot. Understanding from day one, for every child.
